DAY 19 – Speech-to-Text Functionality

Date:16/7/2026

Today, I focused on implementing and improving the Speech-to-Text functionality in my project, **Veltrix AI – Gemini**. The main objective was to allow users to speak their 
instructions instead of typing them manually, making the application more convenient and accessible.

With the guidance of my project mentor, I explored how recorded audio could be processed and converted into text. I worked on connecting the voice input feature with the AI 
workflow so that recognized text could be passed to the Google Gemini API for further processing.

I tested different spoken commands related to creating reminders, mentioning contact names, and scheduling tasks. I observed how accurately the speech recognition process 
converted spoken instructions into text and how Gemini responded to the recognized messages.

I also explored possible limitations of voice recognition, such as background noise, unclear pronunciation, incorrect recognition of names, and microphone quality. These 
observations helped me understand the importance of validating recognized text before processing it through the AI agent.

**Work Performed:**
- Explored the basic working of Speech-to-Text processing.
- Connected recorded voice input with the AI agent workflow.
- Tested different spoken commands related to reminders.
- Worked on passing recognized text to the Google Gemini API.
- Checked the accuracy of converted text.
- Identified possible voice recognition limitations, including background noise and unclear speech.
- Explored the need for validating recognized text before further processing.

**Learning Outcome:**

Through this activity, I learned how speech-to-text functionality converts spoken instructions into text that can be processed by an AI model. I understood how voice input 
can be connected with the Gemini API to create a voice-controlled reminder system. I also learned about common speech recognition challenges and the importance of handling
inaccurate or incomplete voice input.
